02 // THE PROBLEM

GROUNDWATER IS INVISIBLE — ITS IMPACT ISN'T.

Critical challenges in groundwater conservation and depletion management

01 / Aquifer Depletion

Unseen Reserve Over-Extraction

  • Rapid drawdown across agricultural & urban zones.
  • Severe lag in traditional manual well measurements.
  • Subsidence & irreversible storage capacity destruction.
02 / Water Quality Risk

Silent Contamination

  • Rising Electrical Conductivity (EC) & Turbidity levels.
  • pH spikes endangering drinking water & agricultural yield.
  • Lack of continuous temporal contamination tracking.
03 / Blind Decision Making

Reactive Management Failure

  • Policy interventions arrive long after wells run dry.
  • Inability to model future stress under climate variability.
  • Absence of centralized spatial intelligence platform.

10 // GROUNDWATER POTENTIAL MAPPING

MULTI-CRITERIA GROUNDWATER AVAILABILITY ASSESSMENT

Overlaying spatial factors to assess subsurface storage potential

SPATIAL WEIGHTED OVERLAY MATRIX

Assessment Model: Potential Index = f(Rainfall, Soil Permeability, Slope, Land Cover, Drainage Density, Ground Truth)

Topography & Slope

Flatter terrain promotes surface water ponding and infiltration over rapid surface runoff.

Soil Permeability

Porous sandy soils enable significantly higher percolation rates than impermeable clay.

Drainage Density

Lower stream density indicates higher subsurface absorption and storage potential.

Land Cover & Vegetation (NDVI)

Dense vegetation enhances root-zone soil porosity and shields soil from direct evaporation.

11 // RECHARGE-POTENTIAL MAPPING

IDENTIFYING OPTIMAL GROUNDWATER RECHARGE ZONES

Guiding artificial & natural recharge intervention programs

INFILTRATION & RECHARGE FACTORS
  • Precipitation intensity, frequency & seasonal duration.
  • Hydraulic conductivity of surface soil layers.
  • Geological lithology & fracture zone connectivity.
  • Surface slope & surface runoff retention time.
  • Pre-monsoon soil moisture saturation baseline.
ACTIONABLE INTERVENTIONS
  • Check Dam Selection: Pinpoint high-percolation sites for check dams.
  • Recharge Shafts: Locate optimal coordinates for rainwater injection wells.
  • Urban Harvesting: Guide municipal rainwater harvesting enforcement mandates.
  • Watershed Protection: Designate protected high-recharge conservation zones.

14 // GROUNDWATER DEPLETION & STRESS ZONES

IDENTIFYING CRITICAL DEPLETION THRESHOLDS

Categorizing aquifers by depletion speed & recovery deficit

STRESS CLASSIFICATION MATRIX
  • Safe Zone: Stable water table, positive annual recharge balance.
  • Semi-Critical Zone: Drawdown trend detected, extraction equals recharge.
  • Critical Stress Zone: Severe drawdown, extraction exceeds recharge by >20%.
  • Over-Exploited Zone: Irreversible storage decline & subsidence risk.
EARLY WARNING METRICS
  • Rate of Drawdown (m/year): Accelerating annual water depth decline.
  • Recharge Deficit Percentage: Unreplenished volume per extraction cycle.
  • Quality Degradation Rate: Concurrent rise in EC/salinity alongside drawdown.
  • Infrastructure Vulnerability: Threat level to municipal water intake wells.
